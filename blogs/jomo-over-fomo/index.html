choosing JOMO over FOMO

October 13, 2026

jomo > fomo

the joy of missing out.

For the longest time, missing out felt like a bad thing.

Someone's out with friends. Someone's on a trip. Someone just got a promotion, moved to a new city, started something exciting, or is having the kind of life that looks really good on Instagram.

And there you are. At home. Doing your own thing.

Except, somehow, doing your own thing doesn't always feel like enough when you can see what everyone else is doing.

There's always something happening somewhere. Someone to meet, somewhere to go, something to try, something you haven't done yet.

And what if you're missing the one thing you were supposed to be part of?

That's FOMO — the fear of missing out.

It's not always about wanting to be somewhere. Sometimes, it's just the discomfort of knowing that something is happening without you.

You might not even want to go to that party. But the moment you see everyone's pictures, staying home suddenly feels like the wrong decision.

Funny, isn't it? You were perfectly happy until you found out what you weren't doing.

the internet makes it worse

There's a lot of life happening outside our own little corner of it.

Social media gives us a front-row seat to everyone's highlights. The dinners, the holidays, the new jobs, the spontaneous plans, the milestones.

And even when we know we're looking at carefully selected moments, it's hard not to compare.

Their Saturday night looks more exciting than ours. Their career seems to be moving faster. Their life looks fuller.

We start measuring our own lives against things we were never actually trying to have.

And somewhere along the way, being content with our own lives starts to feel suspiciously like falling behind.

Maybe we're not even jealous. Maybe we just don't want to be the person who wasn't there, didn't try it, didn't know about it, didn't have a story to tell.

So we say yes when we want to say no. We go when we'd rather stay. We keep checking our phones when we'd rather be present.

Not because we want to. Because we don't want to miss out.

enter JOMO

The joy of missing out.

I like the idea of JOMO because it changes the question.

Instead of asking, what am I missing?, you start asking, what am I making room for?

Maybe you're missing a night out, but getting a quiet evening to yourself.

Maybe you're missing a trend, but saving money for something you actually care about.

Maybe you're missing an opportunity that everyone else seems excited about, but choosing something that makes more sense for the life you're trying to build.

Maybe you're just staying home on a Friday because you've had a long week, your favourite food is in the fridge, and the thought of putting on real clothes feels like too much work.

And for once, you don't need to justify it.

That's the part I find interesting.

JOMO isn't about convincing yourself that everything you miss is secretly good for you. Some things really are worth showing up for. Some invitations matter. Some opportunities won't come around again.

It's about being okay with the fact that you cannot be everywhere, do everything, know everyone, or experience every version of life available to you.

Every yes takes something. Time, energy, money, attention. Sometimes all four.

And every no creates space for something else.

you can't live every life

I think this is the part we don't talk about enough.

There are so many ways to live a good life.

You could move abroad. Stay close to your family. Build a business. Take the stable job. Travel every month. Save aggressively. Spend more time with your friends. Spend more time alone.

And somewhere, someone is living each of those lives.

You could spend years wondering whether you chose the right one.

But you can't live all of them simultaneously.

Choosing one direction means not experiencing some of the others. That's not necessarily a mistake. It's just what choosing means.

The problem begins when we interpret every unchosen possibility as evidence that we're doing something wrong.

Maybe the life you didn't choose would have been wonderful, too.

You don't have to deny that to appreciate the life you're actually living.

You can wonder what might have happened without treating your present as a consolation prize.

the joy is in choosing

I don't think JOMO means becoming indifferent to the world. I still want to explore, meet people, try things, take chances, and say yes to experiences that scare me a little.

I don't want to stop being curious about what's out there.

I just don't want curiosity to turn into constant dissatisfaction with what's here.

There's a difference between wanting more from life and feeling like your life is never enough.

Maybe the goal isn't to stop missing out.

Maybe it's to stop treating every missed experience as a loss.

To let your friends have their Friday night without wondering whether yours is somehow inferior. To close an app without feeling like you're disappearing from the world. To choose rest without feeling guilty about everything you could have been doing instead.

To make decisions based on what matters to you, rather than what you're afraid of being left out of.

And sometimes, to do absolutely nothing and enjoy it.

Not because you're waiting for something better to happen.

Because this moment is yours, too.

Maybe the real joy of missing out is realizing that you don't have to participate in everything to have a life worth living.

So, what are you choosing to miss out on today?
