loremaxxing: become a person with stories

There's a new way to think about becoming interesting: loremaxxing.

The internet loves turning everything into something to optimize. Your fitness, your career, your appearance, your habits. There's always another version of yourself to work towards.

Loremaxxing is a little different. It's about building a life with stories behind it.

Taking the class you've always been curious about. Going somewhere alone. Getting obsessed with an oddly specific subject. Trying something you're terrible at. Having experiences that eventually become the stories you tell your friends.

Not everything needs to become a skill, a side hustle, or a line on your resume.

Sometimes, you can just do things because you're curious about them.

stop making everything useful

We've become pretty good at asking, What will I get out of this?

Will this help my career? Will it improve my health? Will it make me more productive? Is it worth my time?

These are reasonable questions. Time is limited, and some decisions deserve careful thought.

But when every experience has to justify its existence through some measurable benefit, life starts looking like a very long checklist.

You read only to become smarter. You travel to become more cultured. You pick up hobbies because they make you seem interesting.

Even fun becomes another self-improvement project.

Loremaxxing offers a different question: Would this make for an interesting experience?

You don't need to be good at pottery to take a pottery class. You don't need to become a runner to enjoy exploring a new route. You don't need to monetize your writing or turn every curiosity into expertise.

You can let yourself be a beginner without needing to become anything more.

your life is not a personal brand

There's a funny contradiction here, though.

The moment we turn loremaxxing into a trend, we risk turning it into another performance. Now we're collecting unusual experiences so we can appear interesting. We're not just going to a concert; we're creating lore. We're not just trying something new; we're curating a personality.

And suddenly, even spontaneity has an audience.

But the best stories often come from things that weren't planned to become stories.

The awkward first attempt. The unexpected conversation. The hobby you abandoned after three weeks. The trip that didn't go according to plan. The strange subject you spent an entire Sunday researching for absolutely no practical reason.

You don't have to document every experience or make it part of your identity.

Some things can simply belong to you.

go collect some stories

Maybe the point of loremaxxing isn't to become the most interesting person in the room.

It's to give yourself more things to be interested in.

To follow a curiosity before you know where it leads. To try something without knowing whether you'll be good at it. To occasionally choose an experience that doesn't fit neatly into the person you're trying to become.

Your life doesn't have to be extraordinary every day. Ordinary days are where most of it happens.

But you can leave a little room for the unexpected—for the things you do without a five-step plan or a clear outcome.

Because one day, when someone asks you about your life, it would be nice to have stories that aren't all about what you achieved.

Some about what you tried. What surprised you. What made you laugh. What you got completely wrong.

And the wonderfully pointless things you did simply because you wanted to know what would happen.

Build a life you enjoy living, not just one that looks impressive when you explain it.
